suites after exact guest identity succeeds:

- `TestUbuntuProHighRiskMatrixVM` covers FIPS no-automatic-inverse behavior,
  FIPS/FIPS Updates/realtime-kernel reboot classification, both realtime
  variants, Livepatch incompatibility planning, exact purge input, Anbox Cloud,
  ROS/ROS Updates, USG tooling-only reporting, best-effort restoration,
  post-Check drift, and residual-effect reporting.
- `TestUbuntuProFaultMatrixVM` covers invalid and expired synthetic tokens,
  unavailable/unentitled service, missing endpoint, native lock, network loss,
  deterministic cancellation and timeout, malformed and oversized envelopes,
  graph cycle/unknown-member/side-effect drift, partial failure, service
  restoration, rollback failure, and residual effects.

All four commands passed with guest secret cleanup and libvirt domain, disk,
key, and network teardown:

- `make provider-matrix-vm-ubuntu-pro-20.04`
- `make provider-matrix-vm-ubuntu-pro-22.04`
- `make provider-matrix-vm-ubuntu-pro-24.04`
- `make provider-matrix-vm-ubuntu-pro-26.04`

The earlier `make provider-matrix-vm-ubuntu-pro-secret-canary` run covers the
cross-layer canary scenario. The harness rejects both supported live-token
environment variables before starting a VM, injects only its own synthetic
0600 token, scans bounded guest output and retained failures, verifies guest
credential/test-binary cleanup, and verifies infrastructure destruction.

These results qualify deterministic provider control flow and recovery on real
Ubuntu guests. They do not claim a live Canonical subscription or any entitled
package, snap, repository, kernel, boot artifact, or compliance-tool effect.
Explicit invocation modes and retain/purge native-effect distinctions remain
unadvertised because no durable observation seam distinguishes them.
